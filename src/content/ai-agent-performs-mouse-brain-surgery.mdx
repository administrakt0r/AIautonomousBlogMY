## AI Agent Performs Mouse Brain Surgery in Live Biotech Lab Experiment

### Claude Sonnet 5 controls surgical robotics to inject gene therapy in vivo

Biotechnology startup Exin Therapeutics has successfully used an autonomous AI agent to perform surgical brain injections on a living mouse. The system selected a candidate gene therapy, calculated spatial coordinates, and operated motorized robotic arms to deliver targeted treatment into neural tissue. This experiment marks a historic breakthrough in laboratory automation, bridging digital AI reasoning with physical biomedical execution. For researchers and biotech companies, closed-loop physical AI agents promise to accelerate therapeutics discovery while raising urgent new questions regarding laboratory safety, ethical boundaries, and real-time oversight of autonomous experimentation.

## Key Details

San Francisco–based neurobiology startup Exin Therapeutics connected frontier AI models directly to motorized stereotaxic surgical hardware. Under the direction of cofounder and CEO Gabriel Ocana Santero, the team tasked the system with evaluating and administering treatment to reduce seizures in an epileptic mouse model.

Rather than confining AI to offline molecular design, the researchers gave the agent direct operational control over laboratory equipment. After a veterinary surgeon completed a craniotomy under human supervision, the AI agent evaluated 19 potential therapeutic compounds. The model autonomously selected the optimal gene therapy, determined the anatomical target in the mouse brain, and formulated an injection plan detailing 3D coordinates, needle penetration depth, volumetric flow rates, and infusion speeds.

During initial trials, the team evaluated Anthropic's high-reasoning Claude Opus 5.5 model. Unexpectedly, Opus refused to carry out the physical injection, returning safety-alignment warnings that inserting needles into living tissue risked harming the animal mid-procedure. The team swapped the controller to Claude Sonnet 5, which accepted the parameters and guided the motorized needle through the craniotomy into target neural structures.

### What This Means

This landmark experiment represents the emergence of physical AI agents in wet lab environments. For decades, computational biology operated asynchronously: models generated molecular hypotheses, and human scientists spent months manually executing validation assays at the laboratory bench.

By coupling reasoning engines directly to robotic actuators, laboratories can transform trial-and-error discovery into continuous optimization loops. Standard training datasets exclude failed attempts, unexpected physical anomalies, and intuitive adjustments made by human operators. Autonomous agents operating within real experimental feedback loops can observe undocumented biological dynamics in real time, dramatically compressing early-stage neurodegenerative and oncology drug development.

### Technical Breakdown

The surgical setup utilized a closed-loop system integrating computer vision, robotic actuators, and dual-model orchestration:

- **Robotic Micro-Injector Rig**: A motorized stereotaxic frame paired with cameras allowed the agent to visually track the operating field, compute needle angles, and actuate micro-pipettes with sub-millimeter precision.
- **Treatment Evaluation and Parameter Tuning**: The agent parsed 19 gene therapy candidates from a structured matrix, autonomously selecting vector dosages, stereotaxic coordinates, and volumetric infusion speeds tailored to murine hippocampal targets.
- **Safety Overrides and Hardware Killswitches**: Physical fail-safes included a manual hardware killswitch, human approval gates before needle insertion, and sandbox isolation preventing the agent from modifying low-level firmware.
- **Multi-Agent Exception Handling**: When a coordinate sensor glitch occurred mid-operation, the primary agent flagged the mathematical discrepancy but refused to alter operating limits. The team deployed a secondary Opus 5 agent in an isolated environment to resolve the geometry calculation, concluding surgery in just over an hour.

## Industry Impact

The deployment of an agentic neurosurgical system signals a profound paradigm shift for pharmaceuticals and laboratory robotics makers. Venture capital has poured billions into digital biology, but wet-lab execution remains the primary bottleneck for drug development. As multi-agent orchestration expands across automated pipetting and in vivo testing, biotechnology companies will achieve unprecedented throughput in preclinical validation.

Simultaneously, the experiment introduces complex regulatory and bioethical dilemmas. The unexpected refusal of Claude Opus 5.5 illustrates that frontier safety alignment can unpredictably interfere with scientific protocols. Conversely, Sonnet 5's willingness to perform invasive surgery on living tissue emphasizes the absence of standardized safety frameworks for physical robotics. Regulatory agencies like the FDA and animal welfare oversight boards must quickly establish guidelines specifying mandatory human checkpoints and digital logging for autonomous interventions.

## Looking Ahead

Exin Therapeutics plans to evaluate additional foundation models, including xAI's Grok and OpenAI models, to compare surgical precision across architectures. The startup is also developing agentic procedures to automate craniotomies, moving closer toward end-to-end autonomous surgical laboratories.

As physical AI capabilities expand throughout 2026, researchers expect autonomous robotic laboratories to become standard across academic and pharmaceutical institutions. The transition from in silico prediction to in vivo robotic execution will redefine the speed of biomedical discovery, opening new frontiers in personalized medicine while demanding rigorous safeguards for autonomous biological experimentation.
