## Cognition Acquires Poke to Bring AI Personality to Software Agents

### Bringing friendly, conversational charm and multi-session orchestration to Devin.

In a move that signals a massive shift in how the tech industry values human-AI interaction, the prominent AI coding startup Cognition has acquired Poke, the popular messaging-based AI assistant. The acquisition, valued in the "low nine figures," highlights a growing realization that user experience, personality, and proactive engagement are becoming critical competitive battlegrounds for the next generation of autonomous agents. By integrating Poke's highly conversational and friendly persona into the Devin ecosystem, Cognition aims to transform its flagship coding assistant from a sterile software utility into a persistent, collaborative digital colleague.

## Key Details

The acquisition represents a landmark consolidation between one of the industry's most advanced software engineering agents and a leading consumer-facing AI companion. Poke, originally launched in March 2026 by The Interaction Company of California, has quickly built a dedicated base of hundreds of thousands of active users who have exchanged more than 100 million messages on the platform over the past three months alone. Despite its popularity and its pioneering milestone of becoming the first AI agent approved to run on Apple's Messages for Business platform in June 2026, the startup faced significant financial pressures due to high operational compute costs.

Cognition, whose co-founders Scott Wu and Walden Yan were early angel investors in Poke, stepped in to acquire the startup in a deal valuing the business in the "low nine figures." While Poke will remain active on Apple's platform through the end of the year, next year the combined team will begin experimenting with deeper product integrations. Notably, Poke is slated to leverage Cognition’s cutting-edge software engineering model, SWE-1.7, to execute more complex task orchestration and dramatically improve its execution reliability.

### What This Means

This deal is a clear indication that "raw intelligence" is no longer the sole differentiator in the AI landscape. As foundation models commoditize, the ultimate value of an AI agent is increasingly determined by its interface and how seamlessly it fits into human workflows. For Cognition, buying Poke is not about expanding into consumer travel, health, or scheduling apps; it is a tactical acquisition of a mature interaction layer. Software engineers do not want to work with a rigid, black-box script; they want a co-worker that can understand context, hold a persistent thread of memory, and even inject a bit of humor and personality into long coding sessions.

### Technical Breakdown

The union of Devin’s deep coding capabilities with Poke’s specialized conversational framework addresses several critical technical bottlenecks in the current agentic paradigm:

- **Multi-Session Orchestration:** Currently, Devin is limited to handling a single pull request (PR) at a time. Integrating Poke's agentic orchestration layer will allow users to have Poke coordinate, schedule, and manage multiple parallel Devin sessions simultaneously.
- **Cross-Session Persistence and Memory:** One of the main limitations of current AI coding assistants is their short-term memory constraints. Poke's architecture will help Devin retain context, remember past developer preferences, and maintain continuity across separate coding sessions.
- **Advanced Model Synergies:** Poke will transition its backend task automation to Cognition’s proprietary SWE-1.7 model, unlocking vastly superior performance for the complex scheduling, file management, and personal productivity tasks that represent its most popular use cases.

## Industry Impact

This acquisition will likely accelerate a wave of similar deals as enterprise AI companies scramble to "humanize" their autonomous agents. Up until now, the industry has focused heavily on scaling laws and benchmark scores. However, in a real-world production environment, developer experience (DX) and trust are paramount. By showing that an agent's conversational personality and friendly demeanor are worth a nine-figure premium, Cognition is redefining the metrics of success for enterprise AI. Other major players like OpenAI and Google will be forced to rethink their own stark, transactional interfaces in favor of more integrated, persistent, and engaging user-agent relationships.

## Looking Ahead

As the year wraps up, developers and consumers alike should keep a close eye on how Cognition blends these two distinct product philosophies. If successful, the developer of the future won't just run a command-line tool to generate code; they will text a persistent, witty colleague who coordinates a team of specialized sub-agents behind the scenes. This integration could finally turn the myth of the fully autonomous AI co-worker into a highly practical, conversational reality.
