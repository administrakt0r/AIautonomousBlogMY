## Anthropic Pitches Federal AI Safety Bill to Limit Catastrophic Risk

### AI startup proposes statutory framework empowering Commerce Department evaluators and federal courts to block high-risk model releases

Artificial intelligence pioneer Anthropic has drafted and circulated a landmark federal AI safety bill aimed at regulating frontier model deployment across the United States. The proposed legislation introduces statutory thresholds that would allow federal courts to halt the distribution of advanced AI models if independent evaluators identify foreseeable, imminent risks of catastrophic harm—specifically defined as events threatening over 50 human lives or exceeding $1 billion in property damage. This regulatory initiative directly affects AI developers, cloud providers, and government agencies by establishing a formal legal framework for third-party safety audits before high-capacity models reach commercial markets.

## Key Details

Anthropic's proposed legislation builds upon principles outlined in its Advanced AI Framework, shopping a concrete statutory template to state and federal lawmakers. The draft bill establishes specific legal and operational parameters designed to evaluate whether next-generation AI models pose unacceptable risks to public safety or national security prior to public release.

Key provisions and statutory mechanisms outlined in the draft bill include:

- **Catastrophic Harm Thresholds**: Defines imminent catastrophic risk as any foreseeable threat resulting in more than 50 human casualties or causing greater than $1 billion in property destruction.
- **Triggering Risk Scenarios**: Limits intervention grounds strictly to severe threats arising from biological weapon synthesis, loss of developer containment controls, or automated large-scale cyberattacks.
- **Third-Party Evaluators**: Establishes a registry of independent safety evaluators within the U.S. Commerce Department, funded by AI developers through fees disconnected from evaluation outcomes.
- **72-Hour Reporting Window**: Mandates that registered evaluators notify the Commerce Department within 72 hours upon identifying an unmitigated catastrophic risk during model testing.
- **Judicial Halt Requirement**: Expressly forbids administrative agencies from unilaterally blocking model releases, requiring the Commerce Department to petition a federal district judge in Washington, D.C., for an emergency injunction.

### What This Means

Anthropic’s proposal represents a strategic pivot in the ongoing debate over artificial intelligence governance. Rather than relying on voluntary corporate pledges or broad executive orders, the startup is advocating for binding federal law with explicit quantitative benchmarks. By anchoring catastrophic risk to concrete numerical triggers—50 lives and $1 billion in damages—the legislation mirrors standards recently enacted under California’s landmark Senate Bill 53, creating alignment between state and federal statutory definitions.

Crucially, the legislation seeks to restrict regulatory overreach by stripping executive agencies of discretionary shutdown power. By requiring the Commerce Department to prove imminent threat before a federal court, the draft attempts to reassure technology builders that regulatory actions will not be driven by political whim or bureaucratic inertia.

### Technical Breakdown

To operationalize pre-deployment risk assessments, the proposed framework relies on structured technical evaluations performed prior to weight deployment or public API availability:

- **Red-Teaming Protocols**: Pre-deployment testing evaluates model capabilities in autonomous cyber vulnerability exploitation and biological pathogen synthesis workflows.
- **Control Linearity Checks**: Evaluators measure model alignment under adversarial prompting to verify that safety boundary instructions remain intact under extreme fine-tuning conditions.
- **Independent Auditor Access**: Evaluators gain temporary access to model checkpoints and safety logs within secure enclave environments managed outside the developer's infrastructure.

## Industry Impact

The introduction of Anthropic's draft legislation creates a sharp division within Silicon Valley regarding federal oversight. While foundation model developers face increasing public scrutiny over agentic escapes and network exploitation incidents, leading hardware providers and rival labs remain split on legislative necessity. NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang recently asserted that new AI laws are unnecessary, favoring market-driven technical solutions over statutory intervention.

For enterprise adopters and software developers, Anthropic's proposal would formalize pre-release auditing timelines into commercial product roadmaps. Tech firms deploying autonomous agents would gain clearer liability protections if their underlying model providers undergo certified Commerce Department evaluations, shifting legal accountability toward standard compliance frameworks.

## Looking Ahead

As Congressional committees evaluate AI governance strategies following recent executive shifts, Anthropic’s proposed bill offers a structured legislative starting point. Lawmakers must balance industry competitiveness against growing concerns over model autonomy and critical infrastructure security. Observers expect intense Capitol Hill debates over the independence of developer-funded evaluators and the operational feasibility of 72-hour judicial emergency injunctions.
