## Open-Weight AI Models Match Frontier Labs in Autonomous Hacking

### Benchmark evaluation shows DeepSeek and GLM rival proprietary cyber agents at a fraction of the cost

Autonomous open-weight artificial intelligence models from Chinese research labs have virtually closed the offensive capability gap with proprietary frontier models, successfully breaching simulated enterprise networks in comprehensive security benchmark testing. The empirical evaluation by cybersecurity evaluation firm Vals confirms that publicly downloadable models like DeepSeek and GLM now execute complex, multi-step network intrusions almost as effectively as tightly restricted closed systems, while slashing token expenditure by more than 85%. This inflection point directly affects enterprise defenders, critical infrastructure operators, and policymakers worldwide by fundamentally reshaping the economic and strategic balance of cybersecurity defense.

## Key Details

A rigorous evaluation published by AI evaluation firm Vals reveals that open-weight architectures have crossed a milestone in offensive cyber capabilities. Testing the automated execution of multi-stage cyberattacks across complex simulated environments, researchers measured how autonomous models discovered entry vectors, escalated privileges, and moved laterally without human intervention.

The benchmark exposed stark realities regarding the balance between proprietary frontier models and freely distributable alternatives:

- **Near-Parity Intrusion Success:** OpenAI's unreleased cyber model Daybreak completed 81% of assigned multi-step network intrusion objectives across an average of two attempts. Open-weight model DeepSeek followed closely at a 73% success rate, matching Daybreak's performance on several complex tasks.
- **Dramatically Lower Operational Cost:** Executing a simulated attack workflow required between $17 and $46 in API token fees using OpenAI's GPT-6-Sol. In contrast, running the same routines with DeepSeek cost merely $2 to $3 in tokens—an order-of-magnitude reduction in attack costs.
- **Multi-Network Lateral Movement:** The testing framework evaluated two distinct environments: a public-facing web infrastructure leading into a corporate intranet, and an advanced scenario requiring an initial breach to pivot into a secondary target company's internal network.
- **Accelerated Proliferation Timeline:** The results validate warnings issued in April 2026 by frontier labs such as Anthropic during Project Glasswing, where researchers predicted that gated access to cyber-capable models would only grant defenders a six-to-twelve month head start.

### What This Means

For most of 2026, the artificial intelligence policy debate rested on the assumption that frontier developers could contain dangerous dual-use capabilities through trusted access programs and strict API oversight. The Vals benchmark shatters that containment doctrine. When models capable of autonomous multi-step network penetration can be downloaded, cloned, and executed offline on commodity hardware clusters, API gating ceases to function as a barrier.

Furthermore, the radical cost discrepancy changes the threat modeling calculus for corporate security teams. Adversaries operating automated vulnerability scanners or attack swarms no longer need deep financial reserves. At $2 to $3 per attack run, autonomous reconnaissance and exploitation campaigns can be scaled continuously across thousands of targets simultaneously.

### Technical Breakdown

The benchmarked open-weight models demonstrated proficiency across sophisticated technical attack vectors that previously required bespoke human scripting:

- **Dynamic Environment Fingerprinting:** Rather than executing brittle scripts, the open-weight agents interpreted real-time telemetry from reconnaissance utilities, adapting probing strategies when initial service exploits failed.
- **Abliterated Safety Constraints:** Open weights enable practitioners to remove guardrails via weight ablation techniques, producing uncensored models that willingly generate weaponized payload variants and evade standard heuristics.
- **Autonomous Credential Harvesting:** In multi-network pivoting scenarios, models successfully identified misconfigured trust relationships, parsed internal documentation, and harvested cached authentication tokens.
- **Hardware Portability and Privacy:** Because open-weight models run locally on private GPU clusters, offensive operators—and privacy-conscious internal red teams—can deploy them without generating audit logs or billing trails on third-party servers.

## Industry Impact

The benchmark results force enterprise security operations centers and cloud providers to adapt to an environment where adversaries wield machine-speed automation. Traditional security architectures that rely on human analysts reviewing alerts over hours or days are obsolete when attacks unfold in minutes.

Conversely, the same economic dynamics offer a lifeline to defenders. Security teams can leverage cheap, uncensored open models to simulate adversary behavior continuously across internal environments. As Vals researchers noted, running open-weight defensive agents locally enables automated patch verification, proactive code auditing, and continuous red-teaming at a fraction of the cost of proprietary enterprise APIs.

## Looking Ahead

As open-source models approach frontier capabilities in specialized domains like cybersecurity, policymakers face renewed pressure regarding open-weights governance. However, technical experts warn that legislative bans on model distributions will be difficult to enforce against global open repositories and decentralized networks.

In the coming months, the priority must shift from attempting to restrict model access to hardening the digital fabric itself. Developers and infrastructure operators must accelerate memory-safe code transitions, mandate hardware-backed authentication, and deploy autonomous defensive sentinels capable of matching adversarial AI agents stride for stride.
