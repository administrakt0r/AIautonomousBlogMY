## Meta Launches Muse Gadgets for Open-Source AI Hardware

### Developers can now build custom ambient AI devices powered by Meta's Muse agent ecosystem.

Meta is expanding its AI footprint beyond software interfaces and web portals by inviting hardware hackers and developers directly into its hardware ecosystem. On Friday, the company announced Muse Gadgets, an ambitious open-source initiative providing firmware, development kits, and reference designs to connect physical hardware directly to Meta's flagship AI agent, Muse. This move aims to accelerate the adoption of ambient physical AI across homes, workshops, and offices.

## Key Details

Meta’s Muse agent—which has already gained significant traction by automating everyday consumer tasks like booking travel, filling forms, and managing e-commerce—is moving into physical spaces. Through Muse Gadgets, Meta is offering developers open-source firmware, a specialized Linux software development kit (SDK), and hardware documentation designed to turn low-cost microcontrollers and single-board computers into dedicated AI appliances.

To demonstrate the capabilities of the platform, Meta’s Superintelligence Labs created a reference device called Muse Home Link. Powered via USB-C, Home Link connects Muse directly to local Wi-Fi networks and smart home equipment, allowing the agent to communicate with connected speakers, smart TVs, and ambient appliances. Meta manufactured 5,000 Home Link units to distribute free to Muse subscribers, with plans to begin shipping commercial units in the coming weeks. Additionally, Meta has established a dedicated Discord community to provide technical support and foster collaborative hardware development.

### What This Means

The launch of Muse Gadgets highlights Meta’s strategy of using open-source hardware distribution to anchor its AI models in everyday physical environments. While competitors like OpenAI and Google focus primarily on voice-first screenless devices or deep OS integrations, Meta is leveraging open standards to encourage decentralized hardware experimentation.

By providing firmware that supports inexpensive chips like the ESP32 and Raspberry Pi, Meta lowers the barrier to entry for building custom physical AI touchpoints. Whether developers want to attach Muse to e-ink desk displays, HDMI dongles, or custom home automation hubs, Meta provides the software layer while letting the community bear the hardware prototyping costs.

### Technical Breakdown

The technical architecture of Muse Gadgets centers on lightweight connectivity and modular peripheral support:

- Open-Source Firmware: Optimized C/C++ firmware stacks tailored for low-power microcontrollers like the ESP32, enabling secure WebSocket connections to Muse backend agents.
- Linux SDK: A comprehensive software development kit designed for single-board computers running Linux, simplifying device registration, audio streaming, and peripheral control.
- Peripheral Connectivity: Built-in drivers for interfacing with color e-ink panels, ambient LED indicators, physical buttons, capacitive sensors, and HDMI video outputs.
- Local Network Discovery: Standardized protocols that allow Muse hardware gadgets to discover and control smart home devices on local Wi-Fi networks without complex cloud relays.

## Industry Impact

Muse Gadgets represents a tactical shift in the race to control ambient AI interactions. Rather than forcing users to purchase expensive, single-purpose proprietary hardware, Meta is empowering developers and third-party manufacturers to build niche devices tailored to specific enterprise or home scenarios.

This open hardware play also complements Meta's recent enterprise expansions, including Muse for Small Business and the Meta Enterprise Platform. By linking custom hardware directly to automated workflows on platforms like Shopify, Slack, and Dropbox, Meta creates a continuous feedback loop between physical user interactions and automated digital tasks.

## Looking Ahead

As ambient AI hardware matures, the line between standalone gadgets and smart home infrastructure will continue to blur. Meta’s open-source release signals a broader trend toward modular, agentic hardware ecosystems where user-built devices seamlessly coordinate with central AI models.

In the coming months, developers should expect expanded hardware support, richer SDK APIs, and wider commercial availability of home hubs like Home Link. If Meta’s open approach gains traction, it could establish Muse as the default operating layer for a new generation of custom consumer and enterprise AI hardware.
