## Amazon and Microsoft Drop NDAs for AI Data Center Projects

### Tech giants eliminate secret negotiations to counter mounting local data center bans.

Amazon has officially followed Microsoft in dropping non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) during negotiations with municipal and county governments for new AI data centers. As local opposition to massive cloud infrastructure grows across North America, tech giants are abandoning confidential site selection protocols to mitigate public backlash, prevent legislative moratoriums, and accelerate gigawatt-scale infrastructure deployments. This policy pivot directly affects local communities, municipal planners, power grid operators, and enterprise cloud customers who rely on expanding AI compute capacity.

## Key Details

The decision by Amazon Web Services (AWS) to phase out non-disclosure agreements marks a fundamental shift in how hyperscale tech companies secure land, power, and water rights for artificial intelligence infrastructure. For decades, hyperscalers routinely required municipal leaders, economic development boards, and utility planners to sign strict NDAs before discussing multi-billion-dollar project proposals.

This secrecy enabled tech companies to quietly negotiate tax subsidies, zoning variances, and priority utility access without public scrutiny or competitive interference. However, as the compute demands of frontier AI models have expanded data center footprints into suburban and rural regions, confidential deals have sparked intense community resistance.

- **Policy Adoption**: Amazon confirmed it will no longer enforce or request NDAs when negotiating municipal data center developments, matching a similar policy adopted by Microsoft earlier in 2026.
- **Municipal Opposition**: Cities and counties across the United States, including San Francisco and regions across New York State, have enacted one- to two-year moratoriums on data center permits while environmental and economic impacts are evaluated.
- **Infrastructure Scale**: Modern AI data centers require up to one gigawatt of power per facility—equivalent to the consumption of hundreds of thousands of homes—putting unprecedented strain on regional transmission grids and municipal water supplies.
- **Regulatory Pressure**: State legislators and local advocacy groups have warned that confidential agreements undermine local democracy and conceal the true long-term utility costs imposed on residential taxpayers.

### What This Means

The elimination of NDAs represents a strategic concession by hyperscalers attempting to preserve their multi-billion-dollar AI infrastructure roadmaps. Hyperscale cloud providers have realized that secret negotiations, while historically efficient for securing land and tax concessions, have become counterproductive in the AI era.

When communities discover that a massive industrial data center was approved without public input, the resulting political pushback often leads to blanket permitting freezes and emergency legislation. By moving to open negotiations, Amazon and Microsoft hope to address environmental, water, and power concerns before project proposals spark organized political opposition.

### Technical Breakdown

The transition from stealth site acquisition to transparent infrastructure development involves critical operational changes for cloud engineers and site development teams:

- **Public Utility Impact Studies**: Environmental impact reports, electrical grid capacity analyses, and water usage projections must now be filed in open public records before zoning approvals.
- **Community Grid Agreements**: Cloud providers are forced to negotiate co-located energy generation, such as dedicated solar, nuclear, or battery storage, directly with municipal utility boards.
- **Zoning and Noise Mitigation**: Facility designs must proactively address acoustic dampening for cooling towers, localized substation placement, and architectural integration to comply with municipal codes.
- **Timeline Adjustments**: Public review cycles add an estimated six to twelve months to site selection and construction timelines, altering initial AI cluster deployment schedules.

## Industry Impact

The shift toward transparent data center negotiations will reshape how the entire technology industry plans and deploys physical computing resources. Emerging AI startups, neocloud providers, and rival hyperscalers like Google and Meta will face increasing pressure from municipal regulators to match these transparency standards.

Furthermore, transparency may force AI providers to pay higher energy rates or contribute directly to local grid upgrades. Rather than receiving tax abatements funded by local residents, cloud providers will increasingly need to fund local infrastructure improvements, renewable energy projects, and water recycling facilities to earn operating licenses.

## Looking Ahead

While dropping non-disclosure agreements is a necessary first step toward rebuilding public trust, transparency alone will not solve the underlying physical constraints of the AI boom. Hyperscalers will still face severe bottlenecks in electrical transformer manufacturing, high-voltage transmission capacity, and water rights.

In the coming years, expect tech companies to supplement open negotiations with substantial community investments, including off-grid clean power generation and advanced closed-loop liquid cooling systems. The companies that successfully master public engagement alongside hardware engineering will move fastest in bringing next-generation AI infrastructure online.
