## Trump Establishes Super Intelligence Force for AI Policy

### Federal task force chaired by Jay Clayton aims to maintain U.S. dominance while curbing state-level regulation.

President Donald Trump officially announced the creation of the Super Intelligence Force, a federal task force led by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton. Designed to centralize executive oversight of artificial intelligence development, the strategic body is mandated to protect national interests, accelerate American technological leadership, and prevent local state-level overregulation. This administrative action affects AI labs, cloud infrastructure providers, and enterprise software developers across the United States. By establishing a dedicated federal council, the administration seeks to streamline military and civilian deployments while ensuring American labs maintain a decisive competitive lead over foreign adversaries.

## Key Details

The newly formed Super Intelligence Force represents the formalization of the administration's broader effort to rebrand artificial intelligence under the umbrella term "super intelligence." Announced via Truth Social, the council unites high-ranking federal officials under a shared executive charter.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton will chair the body, effectively assuming the role of federal AI czar. Joining Clayton as vice chairs are several key cabinet and agency heads:
- **Andrew Ferguson**: Chairman of the Federal Trade Commission (FTC).
- **Emil Michael**: Undersecretary of War for Research and Engineering.
- **Scott Kupor**: Director of the Office of Personnel Management (OPM).

The task force has been given a strict 120-day deadline to deliver a comprehensive policy report evaluating national security risks and economic opportunities. In statements to press outlets, Clayton emphasized that the primary threat facing the nation is "not being first" in the technological race. The charter explicitly tasks the group with formulating defensive strategies against threats enabled by autonomous agents, while prohibiting regulatory measures that could stifle commercial innovation.

### What This Means

The formation of the Super Intelligence Force underscores a fundamental shift in how the federal government approaches technology governance. Rather than focusing primarily on safety guardrails or risk mitigation frameworks, the new mandate prioritizes rapid deployment, international market dominance, and administrative harmonization.

For technology executives and artificial intelligence laboratories, this structure offers a direct line of communication to federal policymakers. However, it also signals an aggressive stance against state-level regulations. By framing AI progress as a matter of vital national security—ranking its impact above the Industrial Revolution and the internet—the administration is positioning federal authority to preempt state-level AI safety legislation.

### Technical Breakdown

The operational directive given to the Super Intelligence Force focuses heavily on cloud architecture, compute deployment, and defensive threat modeling against adversarial misuse. As foundation models gain autonomous capabilities, federal coordination across defense and civilian infrastructure becomes crucial.

Key technical mandates assigned to the inter-agency task force include:

- **Federal Compute Coordination**: Establishing unified protocols for allocating compute resources across federal scientific agencies and defense networks.
- **Counter-Agent Defense Frameworks**: Formulating security standards to detect and neutralize AI-enabled cyber threats targeting critical infrastructure.
- **Regulatory Standardization**: Streamlining federal compliance rules to eliminate fragmented state mandates that complicate enterprise software deployments.
- **Workforce Integration Protocols**: Developing federal training benchmarks alongside the OPM to integrate agentic software tools into agency operations.

## Industry Impact

The creation of the task force has drawn immediate reactions across Silicon Valley and Washington. Major tech companies and venture capital firms have largely welcomed the administration's commitment to avoiding restrictive regulatory regimes. Investors anticipate that federal preemptions could reduce compliance friction for startups scaling enterprise agents.

Conversely, public policy advocates and state lawmakers express concern that the charter prioritizes speed and market dominance over rigorous safety evaluations. With recent industry incidents highlighting vulnerabilities in sandbox containment, critics argue that federal policy must maintain independent oversight. Defense contractors and cloud providers stand to benefit significantly, as closer alignment with military units will accelerate federal procurement contracts.

## Looking Ahead

Over the next 120 days, Jay Clayton and the Super Intelligence Force will draft their inaugural strategy report. Industry leaders will watch closely for specific proposals regarding export controls on high-end hardware, federal research grants, and executive actions aimed at curbing state safety statutes.

As global competition in superintelligence intensifies, the establishment of this task force confirms that federal policy will remain focused on maintaining American leadership. Enterprise developers and research labs should prepare for a shifting regulatory landscape where national security and technological velocity take center stage.
