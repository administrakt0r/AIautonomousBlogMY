## OpenAI Gains on Anthropic in Enterprise AI Market Share

### New data from Ramp reveals shifting market dynamics as OpenAI closes the gap on Anthropic among corporate card users.

As major artificial intelligence laboratories approach prospective initial public offerings, newly released financial transaction data provides rare visibility into corporate spending behavior. According to corporate expense platform Ramp, OpenAI has begun rapidly regaining market share against key competitor Anthropic among U.S. businesses in the third quarter of 2026. The shift highlights the volatile nature of enterprise AI adoption, where corporate clients frequently adjust model provider spending based on release cycles, pricing, and regulatory compliance requirements.

## Key Details

Ramp, which manages billing and credit card processing for over 70,000 American businesses spending billions of dollars annually, revealed that enterprise AI spending continues to grow substantially. Overall corporate participation in AI tool purchases among Ramp users reached nearly 56% in July 2026, up from 50% in March.

Anthropic previously surpassed OpenAI in corporate market share on Ramp's platform in May 2026, reaching 41% compared to OpenAI's 39%. By July, Anthropic maintained a lead with roughly 44% market share against OpenAI's 40%. However, third-quarter spending trends indicate OpenAI is currently growing at a significantly faster trajectory, closing the gap behind strong demand for its latest releases.

- **Developer Adoption**: OpenAI's rapid third-quarter growth is heavily attributed to its GPT-5.6 Sol model, which has become an increasingly popular choice among software developers and technical teams.
- **Fable 5 Friction**: Anthropic's premium Fable 5 tier experienced slower adoption due to higher pricing structures and regulatory data retention requirements.
- **Data Retention Mandate**: Anthropic's enforcement of a mandatory 30-day data retention period for Fable users created friction among privacy-conscious enterprise buyers.
- **Expanding Market Size**: Both AI pioneers continue to generate expanding revenue streams as the total percentage of businesses paying for software models continues to rise.

### What This Means

The shifting market share figures demonstrate that enterprise loyalty in the artificial intelligence sector remains fluid rather than fixed. Corporate buyers and software engineering teams are demonstrating a willingness to shift spend rapidly between frontier model providers whenever new capability breakthroughs or pricing adjustments occur. While Anthropic established early dominance in software engineering environments through its Claude series, OpenAI's targeted improvements in developer workflows have successfully re-engaged enterprise decision-makers.

Furthermore, the data underscores how non-technical factors such as regulatory compliance and data governance policies directly impact market traction. Anthropic's requirement to retain enterprise prompt data for 30 days to satisfy compliance frameworks proved to be a notable deterrent for corporate clients with strict data privacy mandates.

### Technical Breakdown

Understanding the drivers behind this spending shift requires examining the architectural and policy differences between current flagship offerings:

- **GPT-5.6 Sol Performance**: Designed specifically for high-speed agentic execution, GPT-5.6 Sol offers low-latency reasoning that reduces operational API costs for high-volume enterprise pipelines.
- **Regulatory Compliance Costs**: European and North American regulatory mandates requiring safety audits led to mandatory logging periods in high-capacity models like Fable 5, introducing privacy concerns for financial and healthcare clients.
- **Multi-Model Orchestration**: Corporate spending infrastructure increasingly leverages model routing tools, enabling businesses to dynamically switch traffic between OpenAI and Anthropic endpoints based on cost and capability.

## Industry Impact

For enterprise technology executives, the Ramp dataset confirms that AI spend is becoming a standard operational line item across more than half of all businesses. However, the lack of long-term provider lock-in suggests that companies are actively managing vendor risk by maintaining multi-cloud and multi-model architectures.

For venture investors and public market analysts evaluating prospective IPO valuations for both OpenAI and Anthropic, the data illustrates that enterprise revenue streams remain volatile. Capital efficiency and sustained model performance advantages will be critical for retaining market leadership as competition intensifies.

## Looking Ahead

As both AI giants prepare for eventual public listings, market observers should expect continued head-to-head competition for corporate software budgets. Key factors to monitor include how model providers balance stringent regulatory compliance with user data privacy, as well as how specialized agentic models influence long-term enterprise retention.
