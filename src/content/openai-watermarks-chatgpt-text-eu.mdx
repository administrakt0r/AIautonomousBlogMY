## OpenAI Watermarks ChatGPT Text in EU to Comply with AI Act

### Invisible textGrain technique balances regulatory compliance and model performance

OpenAI will begin adding an invisible cryptographic watermark to text generated by ChatGPT and Codex for users in the European Union. The move comes in direct response to the EU AI Act's transparency rules, which require AI developers to mark machine-generated output. By introducing its new textGrain algorithm, OpenAI aims to meet European legal mandates while preserving model reasoning quality and conversational fluency.

## Key Details

The EU AI Act transparency rules, which took effect on August 2, require generative AI providers to mark synthetic content so that automated systems can identify machine-generated text, code, and media. In compliance with these rules, OpenAI confirmed that text watermarking will roll out over the coming weeks to eligible ChatGPT and Codex subscribers within EU member states across all service tiers.

For developers using OpenAI's API globally, text watermarking will be available as an opt-in setting for select models starting today, though it remains off by default. OpenAI emphasized that it is not making text watermarking a global consumer default at launch.

Developed alongside academic researchers from the University of Pennsylvania and Yale University, the textGrain method operates without inserting visible characters or formatting changes. Instead, textGrain subtly modulates the model's token sampling probability distribution during generation, biasing word choices using a secret key. Because the watermark lives within the statistical distribution of vocabulary choices, it travels with the text when copied and pasted.

OpenAI reported that internal testing showed no meaningful change in model performance or reasoning accuracy with textGrain active. The company also confirmed that watermarks do not contain user identifiers or personal account metadata.

### What This Means

This rollout marks a shift for OpenAI, which previously held back from deploying text watermarks over commercial and user retention concerns. Internal reports from 2024 revealed OpenAI had functional text watermarking prototypes but delayed releasing them out of concern that users might switch to unwatermarked competitors.

However, the legal enforceability of the EU AI Act has forced frontier AI labs to comply with European mandates. While Anthropic deployed global text watermarks across Claude earlier this summer, OpenAI is choosing a geofenced approach. By restricting mandatory watermarking strictly to EU users, OpenAI satisfies European regulators while insulating its broader global user base.

### Technical Breakdown

OpenAI published a technical report detailing the mechanics behind the textGrain watermarking protocol:

- **Pseudo-Random Token Biasing**: A cryptographic secret key seed splits the model's candidate token options for each position.
- **Statistical Signal Accumulation**: Over a sequence of 100 to 200 words, subtle statistical preferences create a signature detectable by verification tools.
- **Robustness Against Editing**: In stress tests, replacing 10% of words with synonyms reduced detection accuracy from 92% to 66%.
- **Controlled Access**: Initial detector API access is restricted to approved researchers and expert bodies to prevent evasion.
- **Detection Edge Cases**: Short passages under 50 words, math solutions, rigid code snippets, and translated text exhibit higher false-negative rates.

## Industry Impact

OpenAI's deployment of textGrain sets a precedent for how tech companies handle regional AI regulations. With major firms committing to the EU code of practice for synthetic media provenance, watermarking is becoming an enterprise standard in Europe.

However, technical limitations remain. OpenAI cautioned that a missing watermark does not prove human authorship, as text may have been heavily edited or generated by un-watermarked models. Educational institutions and enterprise teams should not rely solely on automated detectors for attribution decisions.

## Looking Ahead

As European regulators begin enforcing the AI Act, focus will turn to how reliably detection tools perform in real-world workflows. OpenAI plans to expand access to its textGrain detector API throughout late 2026 while gathering feedback from European clients.

The outcome of OpenAI's geofenced watermarking strategy will determine whether rival developers adopt regional controls or move toward universal global watermarking standards.
